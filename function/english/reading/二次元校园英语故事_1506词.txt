Sakura Days — A School-Year Story Built from 1,506 Words
《樱日常：用1506个单词写成的一年》

使用说明：本故事只用 english.txt 里的 1506 个单词作为实词，按校园一年的时间线推进，不按字母表顺序排列。人物：Miku（活泼女生）、Haru（邻居发小）、Mika（闺蜜）、Sora（安静的同班男生）、Aoki 老师、Miku 的奶奶，以及后来捡到的小猫 Momo。全篇为简单短句，方便边读故事边记单词。


Chapter 1 — An April Morning 四月的早晨

"Miku, you are almost late again!" Grandma calls from the kitchen. Miku is awake at once. She does not want to be absent on the first day of April. She is able to wash and dress in about ten minutes, although she is not always this active. Above her bed is an article about Australia she cut from a newspaper; she dreams of going abroad one day. An airplane on the advertisement above the desk seems to smile at her.

"Take my advice," Grandma says. "Eat first, and avoid an accident along the road." Miku agrees with a nod, and their agreement is silent as always. She is afraid of nothing except an empty stomach, so she adds bread and an egg to her plate. Her arm aches a little after yesterday's activity, but she is alive and happy, and the ache goes away.

Across the street, Haru is already waiting. "Anyone can see you are in a hurry," he says aloud. "Anyway, hurry, or we will not arrive before the bell!" They walk along the road together. Among the trees, a small animal appears and then runs away. Miku asks about it, but nobody can answer. "It is only a cat," Haru says. Miku does not know it yet, but she will adopt this little cat in autumn and name it Momo. Another day at school is about to begin.


Chapter 2 — The First Class 第一节课

As they arrive at the school gate, the area around them is full of active students. The school art show is in April, and an Asian teacher is putting paper on the board. "Attention, please!" she says. "In August, some Australian friends will visit us from Asia. Anybody who wants to join should ask at the office. All seats are available."

In the classroom, Miku sits between Mika and the window. She is almost asleep because she woke at dawn. Mika is angry about this. "Don't sleep! Sit up and listen!" she whispers. Miku opens her eyes again and looks around. An ancient map on the wall shows Asia, America and Australia, and even a small army of toy soldiers from ancient times. Ms Aoki enters, and all talk stops.

"Today," she says, "we answer one question: what do you want to do after school? Anyone may speak." Mika stands at once. She talks about art. Haru talks about the army and his uncle, a pilot who works near an airport and knows every airline and airplane. Sora, a quiet boy, asks a question in return: "May we write an article instead?" Everyone laughs, but Ms Aoki allows it. "Of course," she smiles. "Learning is not against you. It is an advantage, not a problem."


Chapter 3 — Break Time 课间

The bell rings at last. Before the break, the morning feels long. Miku takes a biscuit from her bag and bites a bit; it is a little bitter, so she gives half to Mika. Beside the window, a brown bird sits on the board outside. Beyond the playground, a big blue building is being built; workers build it day by day, and behind it is a small bank.

Haru borrows a book about bears. "I was born in the countryside," he says, "and my brother once saw a bear near a bamboo wood." Nobody believes him, but he brings a photo the next day: a black bear below a bridge, and a butterfly on its nose. Both Mika and Miku laugh. "You are brave, but your story is bad!" Mika says.

Sora is busy at the blackboard. His background is simple: his family runs a small business, and his father is the boss of a boat shop by the beach. Besides study, he loves baseball and basketball. He can beat any boy in a bike race, but he never brags. "Use your brain," he says, "and bring a bottle of water. We need a healthy body and a bright mind."

The wind begins to blow. A red balloon flies by the window, and Miku watches it rise above the bathroom roof. Breakfast was long ago, and her bowl of beef soup at home seems far away. She takes a deep breath and breathes slowly. "Don't burn out," she tells herself. "The busy day has only begun."


Chapter 4 — After School 放学后

When school is over, Miku walks back home with Mika. They pass a small shop, and Miku decides to buy some butter, a cabbage and a cake for her little cousin. "It is his birthday soon," she explains. "His birth was in this city, and he is only a baby. At the bottom of his basket, I will hide a candy." Mika smiles at this kind behaviour. "You two will become good friends; kindness has a clear benefit."

At home, Miku's bedroom is clean but her bed is not made. She takes a brush and tidies her hair, then checks the bill from the shop. It costs little, because eggs and butter are cheap this week. A blind old man lives at the bottom of the building, and Miku often helps him; today he gives her a coin with a smile. "You have a good heart," he says. Miku's face becomes red. She is not careless; she listens carefully and cares for people.

In the evening, the family sits at table. Her brother is bored by a boring TV show, so Miku cheers him up. "Don't cry over a small cut," she says when he hurts his finger and sees a bit of blood. "Be brave." Dinner is chicken, cheese, cabbage and bread with butter; her mother is a careful cook and the cooker shines. After dinner, Miku covers the leftover food and washes a cup, a bowl and a pair of chopsticks.


Chapter 5 — Chemistry and Chinese 化学课与语文课

Next morning, the classroom is bright. Miku's classmates clean the floor and the ceiling; a chair is broken, and the chairman of the class, a clever boy, calls the teacher. "Take care," he says carefully. "A careless step may cause an accident." He carries chalk and a box of crayons to the teacher's desk.

First class is chemistry. The coach of the science club stands at the centre, and every child sits in a circle. "Today we compare colours," he says. "Check the cups, and correct your notes. Of course, no cheating; a cheat only cheats himself." They mix coffee, Coke and cold water, and the colour changes like a cloud on a cloudy day. "Cool!" the children cry, and they clap. The class captain, a kind Canadian girl, takes photos with her camera.

Then comes Chinese. Miku reads a composition about China, a country with an old culture. She must choose a topic; her choice is a Chinese child who lives in the capital and keeps a cat. She could copy a common article, but she wants to create her own. Her deskmate collects coins and stamps; he shows a coin from Canada and a cap from a college club. "May I borrow your crayon?" Miku asks. "Certainly," he says, and they continue their conversation.


Chapter 6 — Planning the Camp 筹备露营

At five o'clock, the clock strikes five, and school closes. Clouds come, and Miku coughs; she has caught a cold. "Put on your coat," Mika says. "Cotton clothes are comfortable, and this wind is cruel." They count the students in their group: a couple from the music club, the coach, and a crazy boy who can climb anything. The complete number is eight.

"Don't cancel the trip," Haru says. "We can celebrate after the city competition. We may even win!" Sora connects his computer to the screen to show the conditions: the coast is cold, the city centre is crowded, and the camp is beyond a cotton field. "Keep control of your money," he says, "and copy the map. Compare the two roads, and choose the short one."

Miku buys candles for Christmas, because the class will celebrate with a concert, and a cheap ticket does not cost much. She catches a bus back, and on the way she sees a thin man cough in the street. Cancer made him weak, she hears, and her heart cries inside. "Life can be cruel," she thinks, "but small kindness counts." At the corner shop, a couple share a cake by candle light, and she cheers up again. The camp will come; for now, she must complete her composition and correct every line.


Chapter 7 — A Dialogue at the Desk 书桌前的对话

It is a daily problem. After dinner, Miku sits at her desk and opens her diary. She must decide the date of the class play, and the decision is difficult. "Don't dig a deep hole for yourself," Haru says. They have a quiet dialogue; they discuss, and their discussion lasts an hour. Miku can describe her idea in different words, but the difference is small and the difficulty is real.

"I dare not ask the director," she says. "He may discover our plan and divide the class in two." Haru does not doubt her, but he will not depend on any teacher. "Depend on yourself," he says. "Discuss it tomorrow, and do not let fear disturb you." Miku laughs at this dark joke. Her doll falls down, a drawer opens, and a dozen pencils drop to the floor. A dictionary lies beside them.

During December, the days grow short. From her window, in the direction of the school, she can see a long distance of white road. She likes to draw her dream school there: a place that will develop new skills, and the development of every student matters. "A degree is not everything," she writes in her diary. "It is my duty to try." She draws a deep breath, closes the drawer, and shuts the book.


Chapter 8 — A Cold Evening Downtown 寒冷的傍晚，进城去

On Friday evening, Miku and her mother walk downstairs and go downtown. Mother's daughter holds a dry coat and a red dress. The street is dark after six, and a driver drives his truck too fast; "Danger!" a man shouts, for the road is dangerous. A deaf old lady does not hear, so Miku dares to pull her back. "You may die if you run," she says softly. The old lady is not dead, only afraid, and she thanks Miku again and again.

They enter a small restaurant. The dishes are delicious: duck, dumpling soup, and a dry cake. Miku is hungry after the dentist, for the doctor said her tooth was fine but gave her a small drug in a paper bag. "Don't drop it," the doctor said. "Disease does not wait." A little doll sits on the shelf, and a toy drum stands beside it; a dollar can buy both, but Miku has only half a dollar. She does not cry for the dead winter leaves outside; she doubles her money another day.

After the meal, they drink warm tea and watch a girl dance to a drum. A man in a dirty coat begs at the door; Miku's mother gives him a dish of rice. "Nobody should die hungry," she says. On the drive home, Miku dreams of a warm bed. She drops her shoes at the door and hangs her dress. Today she learnt that danger and kindness live on the same street.


Chapter 9 — Numbers Class 数字课

Monday is maths day. "Open your books to page eighty," the teacher says. Miku can count from one to a hundred with ease: eight, eighteen, eighth, eighty; eleven; five, fifth, fifty; four, fourteen, fourth, forty; six, sixteen, sixth, sixty; seven, seventeen, seventh, seventy; nine, nineteen, ninety, ninth; three, thirteen, thirty, third; twelve, twelfth, twenty, twentieth. She can also say a thousand and zero, twice, a dozen, a couple and a double.

"Easy?" the teacher asks. "Then add eleven and eighty, divide by four, and tell me the degree of the angle." Miku finds it easy, and she easily finishes first. Each answer is correct except the eighth one, a silly extra mistake. "Use an eraser," the teacher says. "An excellent pupil may still err." He encourages the class: effort matters more than speed, and education is for everyone, everywhere, every day. Everybody nods; everyone tries; everything feels possible.

Sora sits at the edge of the row. He is the elder brother of twins, and he is kind to the elderly. He especially loves numbers, but electricity and electric machines interest him more. "An engineer must understand energy," he whispers to Miku. After class, he sends an email to an engineer in England, and another to a friend in Europe; his European penfriend writes in simple English. "Even small efforts add up," Miku says, and Sora smiles.


Chapter 10 — Geography: The Wide Earth 地理课：广阔的世界

Geography is on Tuesday. The teacher points east. "To the east, the earth meets the ocean. Europe lies far away, and England is an island country." A map shows Asia, India and the Indian sea, America and Australia, Russia and the Russian plain, Japan and the Japanese islands, China and the Chinese rivers, Canada, and the whole international world. "An elephant walks in Africa, a giraffe eats from a tall tree, and energy comes from oil, coal and electricity," he says.

Miku raises her hand. "May we protect the environment?" she asks. The teacher is glad. "Yes. Empty bottles pollute; we should not empty waste into the river. The environment has no enemy except ourselves." He asks them to examine a photo, give an example, and explain it in their own words. An experiment with a globe follows; it is an expensive toy, but the school can afford it. Miku expects a good grade, and she does not fail. "Excellent!" the teacher cries, extremely pleased. "Your expression is clear; express your ideas without fear."

At the end of class, he sets extra exercises. "I expect everyone to finish. An exam will come, and I will examine each paper." Miku does not excuse herself; she loves the experience of learning. She writes an email to a foreign penfriend, a foreigner she has never met, to exchange ideas. The world feels large, and she wants to go anywhere, to see everything with her own eyes.


Chapter 11 — Exam Week 考试周

Exam week comes at the end of term. Every morning, Miku gets up early, eats an egg, and reads. The subjects are many: English, Chinese, maths, physics, chemistry, history and geography. She keeps an eraser, a pencil, ink and a ruler in her bag. Grammar is hard, so she reads an example aloud, again and ever again; "ever" is a small word, but she has never worked this hard.

"Don't excite yourself," Mika says. "You look tired; rest your eyes." Miku's eyes ache, but she will not end early. She must explain a physics experiment, express an opinion, and pronounce every word well; pronunciation needs practice. Some questions are extremely difficult, and an extra page is given. She does not expect an easy paper; she only hopes her effort will be enough.

On the final day, the teacher enters with the papers. "Begin," he says, "and nobody may leave early, except to see the doctor." Miku examines every line. She will not fail; failure is not her plan. When the bell rings, she gives her paper a final look and walks out into the evening. The air is cool, and she feels empty but free. "Let's eat!" Mika cries. "You deserve an ice-cream!" They laugh, and the tiredness ends.


Chapter 12 — Grandma's Farm 奶奶的农场

On Saturday, the family visits Grandma's farm. Father drives, and Miku faces the window. The farm is not far; fields run for a mile, and a farmer works the land. "The fair is in the factory town today," Father says, "but the farm is quieter." Miku is familiar with this road, for the family comes often; the farm is famous in the family for fresh food. A fan turns slowly in the kitchen, and the cat is fat on milk.

Grandma feeds the chickens. A hen guards her eggs, a frog jumps near the well, and a fox watches from the forest. "Don't forget to close the fence," Grandma says. "A fox may steal a bird." Miku fetches water from the fridge inside, fills a cup, and feeds a lamb. The fisherman grandfather comes back from the river with a fish; he fishes every weekend. "First, fix the net," he says. "Then the fish will not fight free." His feet are wet, but he is fit and strong.

Miku's favourite time is evening. They pick fruit: pears and grapes. A flag flies over the garden, flowers fill the ground, and the floor of the wood is soft with leaves. She has a fever and a touch of flu, so she sits by the fire; the heat is fine on her finger and her foot. "Follow me," Grandma says, "and fetch a fork. Food first, worry after." The table is full: fish, fresh bread, fruit, and a funny cake shaped like a football. Miku feels no fear here; the farm is her favourite place in the future and now.


Chapter 13 — In the Garden 花园里

Sunday morning, Miku works in the garden. The gate is green, the grass is wet, and grapes grow on the wall. She gives the plants water and watches them grow; a golden sun sits above the ground like gold. Grandpa, a kind gentleman, sits in his chair. His grandchild, a little granddaughter, chases a goose; the grandson plays a game with a goat. "Granny wants a glass of water," Grandpa says, and Miku guesses at once. She greets the guests with a smile; the greeting is warm.

A group of children come to the gate. They carry gifts: a small guitar, a toy gun, and a glove for gardening. "Guess my gift!" a girl cries. Miku gets a box of golden paper; inside is a glass bird. "Glad you like it," the girl says. A government officer walks by with a guard; he grades the new road and speaks to a group of workers. The general shop is open, and a man sells grapes by the gram.

In the afternoon, they play games. The boys play football; the girls skip; Sora goes to the gym. A giraffe picture hangs on the wall of the playroom, beside a map of the government buildings. Miku gets a guitar and tries to play; her gesture is silly, and everyone laughs. "Give it to me," a guest jokes, but there is no god of music here, only happy children. When evening comes, they say goodbye at the gate, and Miku waves until the road is empty. She guesses tomorrow will be another good day.


Chapter 14 — A Visit to the Hospital 去医院

One Wednesday, Miku wakes with a headache. Her head is heavy, her neck hurts, and she can feel the heat on her face. "You have a temperature," Grandma says. "We must hurry to the hospital." Miku can hardly stand; she hardly ever falls ill, but illness comes without asking. She puts on a hat and a jacket, takes her handbag, and they hurry out. Human bodies are weak sometimes, however strong the human spirit is.

At the hospital, a kind nurse checks her. A medical doctor asks her to open her mouth, show her ear, and breathe in deep. "Breathe slowly," he says. "Your heart is fine, but flu can hurt the chest." He gives her medicine and clear instructions: take the drug after a meal, rest, and do not run. "Health is a habit," he smiles. "A healthy body helps the mind; helpful rest is not lazy." Miku nods; her knee still hurts from a fall, and her leg is weak, but she will mend.

In the hall, she sees an old lady with her husband. The husband holds her hand; here is a hero of everyday love. "Don't hide your pain," he says. "I hear you, and I will help." Miku feels her own heart beat. She hates to see anyone hurt, and she hopes to help someday. Even death loses to such love, she thinks. On the way home, they buy a hamburger and some ham; Miku is hungry, but she eats only half. "Rest first," Grandma says. At the house, Miku hangs her coat, washes her hair, and falls into bed. Sleep covers her like a warm blanket.


Chapter 15 — Hobbies on Holiday 假期的爱好

When she gets well, the holiday begins. Miku has a hobby: she loves to fly a kite on the hill. Haru loves horses; he can ride a horse at a farm outside town. Mika likes to hide behind a high tree and jump out for fun; she is a happy kid with a loud laugh. Sora would rather lie under a tree, listen to music, and read history; he says history tells the story of human heaven and hell.

"Let's have some fun!" Mika cries. She can kick a ball, jump a rope, and run for a kilometre without rest. "A kilo of apples for the winner!" she jokes. They play near a lake; the water is smooth as glass. They pretend a wooden stick is a king's sword, and Mika is the king of the hill. They know it is only a game; knowledge of small joys matters more than a kingdom.

Miku knocks at Sora's door to invite him. "Come on! Don't just lie there. Keep moving; the key to joy is to keep trying." Sora smiles; he cannot say no to a friend. He keeps his book, takes his keys, and joins. They share juice from a bottle, and Mika drinks July-cold lemon juice. "Just a little jump," Miku says, and Sora jumps, knee-deep in leaves. In the afternoon, they sit in the kitchen; Grandma gives them a knife to cut bread and teaches them how to make jam. "You never know until you try," she says, and they all nod.


Chapter 16 — The Internet Project 网络课题

Back at school in February, the class has a big project. "Use the Internet to find information," Ms Aoki says. "I will introduce the task; the introduction is on this paper. Include pictures, but do not just copy. It is important to show the importance of your topic, and it is impossible to improve without facts." Miku imagines a modern world of machines; she wants to invent a helpful machine, an invention for sick children.

Immediately, the group begins. Sora knows information technology; his keyboard is quick, and he can type a kilometre of text in an hour. "An industry uses iron and metal," he says, "but the modern industry runs on information. The Internet has a huge influence; it can increase knowledge in an inch of time." Miku nods; instead of playing, they work inside the library. Instruction books line the wall, and an interesting article gives them new interest.

They interview a manager online; the interview is short but useful. A scientist introduces himself; his introduction mentions a lab where workers invent new tools. "Don't be afraid to ask," he says. "Invite questions, and let the facts speak for themselves; a fact by itself is worth a page." Miku learns to use a printer, a machine that seems like magic. The project teaches them that knowledge is power, and that the world, for all its size, sits inside a small screen.


Chapter 17 — From January to June 一月到六月

The school year moves month by month. In January, snow falls; in February, the children send cards; in March, the wind is cold; in April, flowers open; in May, the sun is warm; and in June, exams begin. July will bring summer, and the children already dream of it. "Time does not wait," Ms Aoki says. "Use every month well."

Miku wears a jacket and jeans in January, and a light shirt by June. She has a part-time job in a small shop on weekends; the job is simple, but she learns the value of work. She joins the music club, where she can joke, jump for joy, and judge her own singing. "Just enjoy," the teacher says. "Don't judge yourself too hard." A boy plays the violin; another kicks a football; a kid draws a king on the board. They kill time between classes with small games, but never kill a real dream.

One afternoon, Miku knocks at the library door. She wants to know more, for knowledge needs work. The librarian is a kind lady; she helps Miku find a key book, a kilo of paper heavy in the bag. "A kilometre of road begins with one step," she says, laughing. Miku walks home under a bright sky. She knows the year will be long, but she is ready to meet it, month after month.


Chapter 18 — The Library and a Letter 图书馆与一封信

The library is Miku's favourite room. It is large and quiet; lamps give soft light, and not a leaf of paper lies on the floor. The librarian, a silver-haired lady, lays a book on the desk. "Last reader left this," she says. "Don't leave it late; learn at least one page a day, and you will never lose." Miku listens; she has learnt this lesson before.

She picks a book about London: a land of fog, big buses, and a long history. The language is hard at the level she reads, but she does not let it beat her. A line in the book catches her eye: a lion at the gate, a lake in a park, a lazy river, and a loud market. She laughs at a lively tale; loneliness leaves her when she reads. She looks at the long list of titles and chooses a second book, low in price and light to hold.

"I will lend you a pen," the lady says, "but do not lose it; I never lend twice." Miku writes a letter to her penfriend. Her handwriting is tidy; she does not litter the page with mistakes. She writes about her life, her school, a lecture she heard, a leader she admires, and the labour of love that learning takes. "Please write back," she ends. "I leave for camp soon, but the library will hold my mail." She feels lucky; luck, she thinks, follows those who try. After lunch of soup and bread, she locks the letter in her bag and runs to class, loud and happy.


Chapter 19 — Morning Market 早市

Saturday morning, the market is busy. Miku and her mother make a list; the main thing is meal food. "Many things are cheap today," Mother says. Men and women move between the stalls; a madam in a fine coat buys a magazine; a man sells mail boxes; a manager from a big company manages his shop with a smile. "Can you manage the bag?" Mother asks, and Miku nods.

They buy meat for a meal, milk, a map of the city, and a model ship. A mark on a metal cup shows the price; the method is simple: weigh it, pay, and go. A metre of silk cloth catches Miku's eye; it might make a pretty shirt. "What does this mean?" she asks, and the seller explains the meaning of the marks. They meet a family member near the menu board of a small restaurant; meeting friends at the market is a morning joy.

Miku mentions a film; her mother mentions a movie too, and they laugh. A monitor plays music; a modern song from last month. A mirror hangs at a stall; for a minute, Miku sees herself and smiles. She does not miss breakfast; she buys a warm cake. "Don't mistake salt for sugar," Mother warns, but the mistake is not made. They watch a monkey show, look at a motorcycle, and buy a mouse toy for the cat. Most of all, Miku loves the moving, noisy, living crowd. She must mind her money, but the morning is worth every coin.


Chapter 20 — A New Neighbour 新邻居

In November, a new family moves next door. "Their name is the North family," Grandma says. The north wind is cold, and a northern bird sits on their fence. Miku takes a plate of noodles and knocks; a nice woman opens the door. "Come in," she says; her voice is noisy with cheer, but not unkind.

A national newspaper lies on a box, but only the father is home; he works for a national park and loves nature. "Natural places need our care," he says. He has nearly no furniture yet; boxes stand near the window, and a notebook lies on a nearly empty shelf. "It is necessary to rest," Miku says. "You need not hurry; nobody works at noon." The man nods; he is nervous about the new city, and his neck is red from the cold.

His daughter, a girl of Miku's age, hides behind him. She has neither brother nor sister, and she is new; she knows nobody. "Don't be nervous," Miku says. "I never bite. I noticed your note about a music club; I can take you there." The girl's nose is red, but she smiles a small smile. She nods, and her nod is the start of a friendship. "Number nine bus goes to school," Miku adds, "and I will wait for you tomorrow morning." When Miku leaves, the house is no longer silent, and a new note of joy fills the air.


Chapter 21 — The October Trip 十月的旅行

In October, the class takes a trip. "Get on the train at the old station," the officer at the office says. He offers tickets; the offer is kind. Our teacher checks each passport, for the railway runs to the ocean. "Don't get off at the wrong stop; once we pass the oil works, we are near." Each pupil carries an object: a bag, an overcoat, an orange, and oil for dry skin. The train opens its doors, and we pull ourselves up; only one bag is our own, and its owner must hold it.

Outside, the sea opens wide. Other children look out; over the water, a plane rises. "Keep order in the line," a passenger officer says, "or someone may fall." We sit in order; the operation of the train is smooth. We pass a palace on a plain, a small post office, and a field of potatoes. An old man sails a boat; he has sailed since he was a boy. The train runs on, and we are off on holiday, glad to be out of the classroom.


Chapter 22 — A Picnic by the Sea 海边野餐

At the sea, the sand is pale. We pass a park and find a place for a picnic. Parents come; a parent paints a picture; a pair of pupils pick flowers. A package holds pancakes, pork, potato pie, salad and a pear; a plastic plate, a paper cup, and a pocket full of salt. We put food on plates; a pretty pink purse sits on the path, and its owner is a poor old lady. "Pardon me," she says, polite and pale. Miku returns the purse; the lady praises her, and the praise makes Miku proud.

A pilot shows his passport photo; a pioneer of the first plane points to the sky. "A planet is a world," he says, "and a plane is only a small machine." We plant a tree in the park; plants grow in the soft ground. Children play ping-pong and table tennis on the playground; a piano plays, and people sing. Plenty of food, a piece of pie, a spoon of sugar; the pleasure is perfect. We post a postcard while the postman passes. "Perhaps the best price of a holiday is joy," Miku says, and everyone agrees. A phone takes a photo of every person, for personal memory.


Chapter 23 — The School Play 校园剧

The autumn programme includes a play. The story: a proud president, a brave queen, a prisoner in a dark prison, and a polite policeman. "Practise every line; practice makes perfect," the teacher says. The pupils prepare; a primary class presents a song; the president of the student club prints programmes. "Don't pretend to know your lines," the teacher warns. "Pronounce each word; proper pronunciation matters. Progress step by step; produce your best, and protect your voice."

A boy plays a prisoner; another is a police officer. They pull a rope, push a stone box, and pretend to punish a bad man. "The purpose is not to prize power," the teacher says, "but to show why we protect the weak. Probably every person here can be a hero; a problem is only a problem." A purple curtain rises; private notes lie in a pocket; a prize waits for the best actor. Miku plays a small part, but she is proud to stand on a public stage. "Promise me you will not freeze," Mika says. "I promise," Miku smiles, and proves her courage that night.


Chapter 24 — Rain and Records 雨天与录音

The rainy season starts. Rain falls; Miku wears a raincoat and raises it over her head. Rather than run, she walks; she would rather be a little late than risk a fall. She reaches the library, ready to read. "I realise now," she tells Sora, "that reading is a real rest." She has received a recent letter; she received it yesterday. Her penfriend writes of a rabbit race, a radio programme, and a recorder she bought to record songs.

Sora refuses to rush; he will not refuse a friend a favour, but he relies on reason. "Relax," he says. "Rely on yourself, remain calm, and remember the facts. Repair a mistake, repeat a line, reply to a letter, report a problem, and respect the result." Miku nods; research requires patience. She reviews her notes, retells a riddle, and returns a book. Rich rice soup waits at the restaurant; the rich man at the next table rides a fine horse, but he is not happier.

After school, a ring of light breaks through the clouds; the sun rises above the river road. A boy rings a bell, rises on his bike, and rides round the rock. A robot toy rolls a rope; a red rose grows by a row of houses. "Right this way," Miku says, ruler in hand; she picks up rubbish, for rules rule the classroom, and a rubber erases a wrong line. They run home, rush up the stairs, and laugh in the rain.


Chapter 25 — Sports Day 运动会

September brings Sports Day. The sky is blue; not a sad cloud is seen. "Safety first," the head says; safe play matters, and the safety of every student is the rule. Saturday is sunny; the school sells salad, salt plums, sandwiches and sausage; a sweet smell rises. Miku wears a scarf, a shirt, shorts, soft socks and shoes; her sweater is in her schoolbag. She saves her strength; to save energy, she sits for a spell.

The first race is the hundred-metre run. Runners stand at a set line; the starter shouts, and they shoot forward. A serious runner sets a fast speed; a shy boy shakes; his shoulders are tight. "Shake hands before a match," the teacher says, "and show respect." Miku scores second; her score is high. She should not feel shame; silver is a fine prize. A silly boy shouts; a strict teacher tells him to shut his mouth and sit silent; silence falls, then smiles.

On the side, children see a sheep from the school farm, a snake in a glass box, and a small silver bell. A girl skates past; another sings a song; someone's skirt sweeps the ground. The sky shines; students sit in a square, stand in lines, and stamp their feet. "Step by step, start strong, stay steady," the coach calls. A strange stranger takes photos; a steel flagpole stands like stone. The standard is high, but every student studies hard, and stupid mistakes are few. Sports Day is a sweet success.


Chapter 26 — The School Shop 校园商店日

After Sports Day, the class runs a small shop. "We sell smoothies, soup, sour sweets, sugar cakes and strawberry pie," says the monitor. The social-study teacher speaks of society: "A social person serves others; a servant serves, and service is noble." A soldier visits; he has served since he was young, and he speaks simply, without a sound of pride. Somebody asks a question; someone passes a spoon; something sweet is given to every guest.

"Spend less, spare more," the teacher suggests; the suggestion is wise. A speaker gives a short speech; special songs play; a boy can spell long words at speed. The spirit of the day spreads; sport and study stand side by side. A stamp stand sells star cards, in the spring-fair standard. They sweep the floor after; the supermarket gives them sugar; supper is soup and bread. "Suppose we support each other," Miku says, "we will succeed; success is sweeter shared." The successful day ends with a surprise: a swing of balloons, and a symbol of the school in silver.

Suddenly, a storm comes; sudden rain hits the street. A stream rises near the station; the ground is soft. A student slips; Miku supports him; he suffers a small hurt but smiles. "Such is life," the teacher says; suffering passes. Sunday is sunny; they swim at the pool; swimming is Miku's summer joy. The surface of the water shines; they swing, they shout, they are simply glad to be alive.


Chapter 27 — Snow, Sleep and Stories 雪、睡眠与故事

Winter comes early; snow falls on a snowy night. Miku sleeps late; she is sleepy until a shower wakes her. She soaps her hands, smooths her hair, and smells the sweet air; smell can wake a person faster than sound. Momo, the slow small cat they adopted in autumn, sleeps on the shelf; its size is tiny, its skin soft. "Don't smoke," a sign says; smoke smells sour and soils the sky. A snake of road winds south; the southern hills are white.

At school, the situation is simple: since September, every single pupil has read six books. Miku's sister, a single-minded girl, sits beside her; similar sisters often share the same skirt size. They learn a skill: to skate on ice, to spell, to sing, and to speak without fear. A subject Miku loves is history: the story of a kingdom, a stolen sword, steel soldiers, and a star that led a traveller home. A stupid thief tries to steal; strict law stops him; the moral is clear.

In the evening, Miku stays home. She sorts her stamps, stores old letters, and studies by lamp light. She still has homework; a sticky note sticks to her book. Her stomach rumbles; supper is soon. She sweeps her room, folds a sock, and hangs a skirt. Sometimes a song sounds from somewhere next door. She smiles; school is hard, but the small days shine the brightest. She shuts the light, slides into bed, and sleeps; tomorrow, the story will continue.


Chapter 28 — A Trip to the Old Town 古城之旅

On Thursday of the tenth week, the class takes a trip to an old town. Miku's team takes a tape, a task list, and a tent for the night. They take the train; the traffic is thick, but a tall teacher tells a tale, and time runs fast. "Take your ticket," the teacher says, "tie your shoes tight, and do not throw things." Through the window, a tiger poster, a thin cat, and three tall towers appear; the town sits towards a tall tower by the river.

At a long table, they taste tea and tomato soup. Miku is thirsty, and her tongue is dry. A tool box, a ton of bags, and a toy truck stand by the tent. Her tooth hurts; toothache is terrible, so she uses a toothbrush and toothpaste and washes with a towel. "The true test of travel is patience," the teacher says. A text book says the town has a thousand years of trade; traditional music plays in the street, and a tourist on tour records it. The theatre stands at the top of the town; the actors teach the team a song, and they thank the players.

They translate an old thread of story; training helps, for they train every Tuesday. Trouble comes: a truck blocks the road, Miku's trousers get wet, and she tears her sleeve. "Don't cry; a tear will not help," the teacher says. "Try; the truth is, you will be fine." They turn a corner and climb twenty stairs; twelve steps, then the twelfth, then twenty, and the twentieth opens a wide view. Though tired, they are glad. Tonight they sleep in the tent together; tomorrow they travel home, with stories to tell their classmates.


Chapter 29 — Under the Wide Sky 广阔天空下

Use an umbrella under the grey sky; unless it rains, an umbrella is only weight. Miku understands now; she used to forget it, but now she finds useful tools usual, and she usually packs light. Underground trains run under the city; upon the stairs, upstairs and down, people hurry to the university. "Use your time well," her father says, "until every minute has its value."

In the spring vacation, the family visits a village. Various vegetables grow in the fields, green and fresh. A video camera rolls; a visitor with a violin plays, and the voice of the violin is soft. They visit an old house; another visitor from the university talks of victory in a volleyball game. Miku values the quiet; the village has no ugly noise, only birds and wind. She will not forget this vacation; various small joys make life rich, and she would not trade them for anything.


Chapter 30 — A Week of Weather 一周天气

Wednesday is wet; warm wind blows; Thursday is windy; the weekend is warm. Miku waits by the wall, wakes early, and walks to school the whole way. "Wear a coat," Grandma warns; she warns twice, for the weather can change in an hour. Miku washes her face; she does not waste water; she watches the window and the wet wheels below. A weak old man weighs wheat; his weight is light, but his wealth is kindness. "Welcome," he says, "well met."

To the west, the western hills are white in winter; a whale poster hangs in the west hall. Whatever the weather, whenever Miku can, she walks; where she goes, she wonders which way the wind blows. "Whether white or wide, the world is wonderful," she writes. A wise woman, a farmer's wife, gives her a cup of fruit wine and a whole wheat cake; whose bread is this? It is hers. She wishes to win, and knows a winner needs will, not luck. Without fear, with friends, she walks on. A wolf in a winter tale, a woman in a wood, a word in a book: stories are work, and workers of words write whole worlds. Miku would not cry over a small wound; she writes the wrong lines again and worries no more.


Chapter 31 — Year End 年末

Yesterday was the last day of the year. In the yard, yellow leaves lie thick; yet young children run about, and your own heart runs with them. "Help yourselves," Grandma says, "and believe in yourself, Miku." A year is short; years pass fast. Miku writes her yearly note on a small card: zero fear, zero waste, and a heart ready for tomorrow. She is young, and the wide world is waiting.


Chapter 32 — The Culture Festival 文化节

The autumn festival is fantastic. At the front of the flat playground, a flower gate rises; the class will paint a panda on a board and print small cards. "Act well; every action counts," the monitor says. An American exchange student, a friendly girl, helps; she can communicate with anyone, and good communication can connect hearts. We sell noodle bowls, a chopstick set, hot hot dog rolls, pancake stacks and grape juice; the sale is busy, and a lot of guests come.

A classmate will collect tickets at the door; another will cover the stall with a blue cloth. "Don't cross the line," he says politely. Across the road, the church choir sings; the cinema club shows an old film. A camel toy from a century ago sits beside a lovely doll. "Allow me," a boy says; "I advise you to take this chance." Miku has to agree; she cannot accept fear in herself, and she will not let either fear or tiredness win. Friends encourage one another with a small exercise of trust. Basic skills matter, but the beautiful feeling of teamwork matters more.

We must feed the guests; Grandma says to hang the signs high. No one knows what will happen next; maybe a mad rush comes at noon, but no one may force a guest to buy. A humorous boy tells jokes; people laugh. Housework at a stall is hard; Miku does not mind. She gives her address to a penfriend, will mention her hometown, and sends a message by telephone. The television in the hall shows the news; none of it is sad today.


Chapter 33 — A Walk and a Talk 散步谈心

After the festival, on a quiet weekday, Miku and Sora walk. They pass a pond, a pet shop, and a small store. "I think," Sora says, "that ability is built, not given. You must reach for it, raise your hand, and receive both praise and regret. Do not regret a try; recite a poem if you are nervous." Miku smiles; a poem about peace, a pleasant passage about the moon. "What a pity the day ends," she says. "I shall remember it."

They sit; several seats are empty. A patient old man, a retired worker, sits with his grandparent and a grandchild. He seems kind; he seldom speaks, but he can fix a wheel and a bicycle tail light. "Be patient," he says; "quality takes time; a quarter of an hour is quite enough." His wife, a former secretary, sorts stamps; the state post office is near. They talk of marriage: they will marry for fifty years; honesty is their honour. "Honestly," the wife says, "to love is to provide for another, and to prevent sorrow before it comes."

Sora raises a point. His theory of learning is simple: separate big tasks into small parts; search for facts; review and retell, until the total is clear. "A report may require facts; relations between people require heart. Do not suffer alone; suggest an idea, for a suggestion helps." Miku nods. "I wonder whom we will become. I want a role that helps people." Her shoe is loose, her shoulder is tired, but her sight is clear, and the future will spread wide like open space.


Chapter 34 — The Mountain and the Museum 山与博物馆

In spring, the class climbs a mountain. At the height of the path, Miku can see the whole town: the hotel, the museum, the toilet house, and the population below, ninety percent of the town in one sight. A popular song plays; the wind will shape the clouds. "I am certain," Miku says, "that change will come; I hope to catch it." She can imagine such a day years later. "I tell myself to be brave," she whispers.

They knock at a mountain house; a neighbor from town, now a keeper, is glad to lay out tea. His license to run the house hangs on the wall; a lock and key sit beside a treasure box. He helps lift a heavy bag, and he will warn them about storms. A notice on the door says the path is safe; treatment for a small hurt waits inside. A murder story in a book frightens them for a minute. "Sorry," the keeper laughs, "that book is not for children; it is sort of scary." A smart guide, a soap smell, each clean stair: the small house feels like a treasure.

After climbing down, they sail a small boat on the lake. A party waits at school: a present for every guest, songs, and a pound cake. Miku stands in position for a photo; the period of the school year nears its end. "Enjoy oneself," the teacher says, "and learn for oneself, too." Miku picks a smooth stone. "This small stone is mine," she says. She is sorry to leave, but the story does not end here.


Chapter 35 — Lunch Break Secrets 午休的小秘密

At noon, the middle of the day, Miku sits in the middle of the classroom. For a moment, the room is quiet; Mika reads quietly, and Haru eats a sandwich and a vegetable pie. "I really love quiet moments like this," Miku says. "Really, normal school days are great, and nobody here need feel lonely."

Sora will call the chess club to order; he can lead the club, and a calm mind can master any game. "Chess teaches sense," he says. "A sharp player must form a plan, and a clear sentence can serve a thought better than a shout." He begins to serve tea, for to serve a friend is a small joy.

"Tell me a secret," Mika says. Miku thinks, and her thought is simple. "I hate war," she says, "and I hate lies. An honest heart is the best form of courage; be honest, even when a condition is hard." With a smile, she can hit a soft beat on the desk, or else a small fear may grow. She makes a wish: "I wish everyone well." "Then prove it day by day," Sora smiles, "and great days will come."


Chapter 36 — Epilogue: A Seat by the Window 尾声：窗边的座位

On the last day, Miku takes a seat by the window. The days of a school year seem long while they pass, yet the year itself seems short in memory. A warm relation between friends is the true treasure of these days; small, simple joys satisfy the heart more than any prize. Outside, the wind moves the flowers, and a new season begins.
